Sunday, November 29 · Pleasure Island

🌿 Carolina Beach & Kure Beach Forage Walk

A relaxed coastal day with the family: two easy trails, a lunch break, and a lot of plant spotting. It's flat, sandy, and good for all ages.

🥾 About 5.5 miles total⏱️ Around 5 hours with lunch📈 Flat and easy💵 Free day use🐕 Leashed pets OK
⚠️ Look, don't pick, on these trails. Carolina Beach State Park and Fort Fisher are both NC state park land, where state rules protect plants from removal. Treat today as an ID safari: photograph everything and log it to iNaturalist. Never touch the Venus flytraps. Poaching them is a felony in North Carolina. For actual harvesting, see the legal picking options below.

🗓️ The plan

9:00 AM

🌲 Sugarloaf Trail, Carolina Beach State Park

This 3-mile loop is the best sampler on the island. It runs through evergreen forest, past limesink ponds and along the Cape Fear River, then climbs over Sugarloaf Dune, an old landmark that was used as a navigation point. It then passes through longleaf pine savanna and cypress swamp.

  • Look for: yaupon holly thickets 🍃 (the easiest win of the day), sand live oak, longleaf pine, and wax myrtle, which has fragrant leaves.
  • Kid activity: a contest to see who spots the most yaupon first.
📍 Start at the marina or the Flytrap Trail parking lot · Orange circle blazes · About 1.5 to 2 hours
11:00 AM

🪤 Flytrap Trail

This is a short half-mile loop through pine savanna where carnivorous plants grow, including Venus flytraps, pitcher plants and sundews. It's wheelchair and stroller accessible, so it's a nice add-on if anyone's legs are tired.

📍 Flytrap Trail lot at the end of Nature Trail Lane · Orange diamond blazes · About 20 to 30 minutes
12:00 PM

🍤 Lunch break

Head into the town of Carolina Beach or drive south to Kure Beach for lunch. Both are just a few minutes away on US-421.

1:30 PM

🌾 Basin Trail, Fort Fisher State Recreation Area

This flat 2.2-mile out-and-back trail runs through maritime forest and salt marsh. It passes the WWII bunker where the "Fort Fisher Hermit," Robert Harrill, lived for 17 years, and ends at an observation deck overlooking Zeke's Island.

  • Look for: wind-sculpted yaupon and live oak in the maritime forest, plus marsh grasses.
  • Bonus: the Fort Fisher State Historic Site earthworks and the NC Aquarium are right next door if the group wants more.
📍 1000 Loggerhead Rd, Kure Beach · Trailhead by the flag pole near the visitor center · About 1 to 1.5 hours
3:00 PM

🌅 Beach walk and wind-down

Fort Fisher has about five miles of beach. Finish with a stroll at the pedestrian end, then go back to the map and log your finds.

🔎 What you'll likely see in late November

🍃 Yaupon holly

It's evergreen and everywhere in the maritime forest. Look for small oval leaves with rounded teeth and smooth gray bark. Its red berries are toxic.

Guaranteed sighting

🍊 Persimmon

It's late in the season, but check sunny edges for leftover orange fruit and the blocky "alligator" bark.

Possible

🧅 Wild onion

New grassy greens start appearing in late fall. Do the smell test: if it doesn't smell like onion, it isn't one.

Possible on edges

🍇 Muscadine and 🫐 blackberry

They're mostly dormant now, which makes it the perfect time to learn the vines and canes so you know exactly where to return next summer.

ID practice

🪤 Venus flytrap and pitcher plants

These grow wild in only a small area of the Carolinas. Photograph them, but never touch or collect them.

Protected

🌳 Sand live oak and longleaf pine

They're not food, but they're great landmark trees that tell you what habitat you're in.

🛟 Safety

Never eat anything you haven't positively identified, and remember that yaupon berries are toxic. Stay on the marked trails around the flytrap savanna. Do a tick check afterward. There's no swimming area at Carolina Beach State Park because of strong currents. In an emergency, call 911. The nearest hospital is Novant New Hanover Regional.
